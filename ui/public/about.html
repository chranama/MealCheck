MealCheck

About

A second check for meal plans.

MealCheck helps you review a meal plan before you rely on it. Paste a concise, ingredient-level plan and MealCheck returns a clear result with the details that need attention.

What It Checks

  • Whether the text is specific enough to verify as a meal plan.
  • Whether meals, foods, quantities, and days can be understood.
  • Whether declared allergens or excluded foods appear in the plan.
  • Whether nutrition limits and prep-safety expectations need attention.

What Works Best

MealCheck works best with plans that list each day, each meal, the foods or ingredients in that meal, and approximate amounts such as cups, ounces, grams, tablespoons, teaspoons, or servings.

For multi-day plans, use clear labels like Day 1, Day 2, and Day 3, then list breakfast, lunch, dinner, and any snacks under the right day.

A vague idea like "eat healthier this week" is not enough. A plan with breakfast, lunch, dinner, ingredients, and portions gives MealCheck something concrete to review.

How To Read Results

A pass means the plan did not trigger the configured checks. A warn means something should be reviewed. A block means the plan has a clear issue that should be fixed before use.

The report calls out the specific meals, foods, or assumptions that led to the result.

What It Is Not

MealCheck is not medical advice and does not replace a clinician, dietitian, or food-safety professional.

Use it as a structured review step, especially when a plan was drafted quickly or needs a clearer safety and nutrition check.

MealCheck is a verification aid. If a meal plan affects a medical condition, allergy risk, pregnancy, childhood nutrition, eating disorder recovery, or another high-stakes situation, review it with a qualified professional.
